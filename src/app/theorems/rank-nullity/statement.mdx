For a linear map $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$,

$$
\dim \ker(T) + \dim \mathrm{im}(T) = n.
$$

The **null space** $\ker(T)$ is the set of input vectors that $T$ sends to zero.
The **column space** $\mathrm{im}(T)$ is everything $T$ can output. The theorem
says these two dimensions always sum to the dimension of the input space — no
matter how degenerate $T$ is, what it loses in the null space it preserves in
the image.

Try editing $M$ in the bottom-right panel. As you change entries, watch the
**yellow null space** in the domain and the **blue column space** in the codomain
breathe in opposite directions: making $M$ more degenerate grows the null space
and shrinks the column space, but their sum stays equal to $n$.
